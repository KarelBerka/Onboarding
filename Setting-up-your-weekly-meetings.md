# Weekly Progress Meetings

It is essential to us that you feel supported, guided, and never left to struggle alone during your research in the lab.

To keep track of your progress, address technical bottlenecks, and plan your goals, we will meet or touch base weekly. This document details how to set up and prepare for your weekly progress updates, along with the motivation behind our questions.

> [!TIP]
> These updates are for *your* benefit as much as for the lab lead. They help organize thoughts, celebrate small wins, and flag issues early before they become roadblocks.

---

## Setting Up Your Progress Log

We keep track of weekly updates using a markdown log file in this repository. 

1. Create a folder named after you in the `meetings/` directory of this repository (use `lowercase-hyphenated` format, e.g., `meetings/john-doe/`).
2. Inside that directory, create a file named `progress-log.md`.
3. Copy the template below into your file.

### Progress Update Template
```markdown
### Date: [YYYY-MM-DD]

#### 1. What did you achieve this week?
* [List your scientific progress, code written, papers read, or debugged issues]
* [It is ok if this list is short—celebrate all progress!]

#### 2. What did you struggle with?
* [Note any roadblocks: failing simulation, buggy script, unclear paper, etc.]

#### 3. What is your plan for next week?
* [List your target goals for the coming week and estimate the time they will take]

#### 4. Who did you help / Who helped you this week?
* [Recognize collaborations and support within the department]

#### 5. Where do you need help or input from Karel?
* [Be specific: need feedback on a draft, help setting up a docking run, cluster access issues, etc.]

---
```

---

## Preparing for the Meeting

Before our scheduled update each week:
1. Open your `meetings/your-name/progress-log.md` file.
2. Paste a copy of the template at the **top** of the file (keeping the most recent updates first).
3. Fill in the sections.
4. Commit and push your changes to your branch or submit a pull request so **prof. Karel Berka** can review it.

During our discussions, we will go over these points, add comments, and help resolve any roadblocks you have listed.

---

## Motivation Behind the Questions

* **Achievements:** Research is a long game, and milestones like papers or theses take months or years. Tracking weekly successes helps you recognize your steady progress and boosts morale.
* **Roadblocks:** Writing down what you struggled with helps us debug faster. It is completely normal to get stuck on computational setups (GROMACS, Amber, python packages)—identifying these early saves time.
* **Future Plans:** Estimating how long tasks will take builds crucial project management skills and ensures you set realistic, healthy goals.
* **Helping Each Other:** Science is collaborative. Acknowledging who helped you and who you helped reinforces that the CDL is a team that thrives on mutual support.
* **Specific Requests:** Asking for specific help (e.g., "Review lines 40-60 of my script" rather than "It doesn't work") helps Karel give you targeted, efficient advice.
