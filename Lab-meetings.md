# Lab Meetings

As our research group grows, sharing computational techniques, domain expertise, and troubleshooting tips becomes increasingly important. Our weekly lab meetings are designed to be a collaborative space where everyone can learn from each other.

* **When:** Every **Friday at 8:30 AM** (CET)
* **Where:** **MS Teams** (with a hybrid option in room **3.009** at KFC UPOL)
* **Attendance:** All active members of the Chemical Data Lab (Bc., Mgr., Ph.D. students, and Post-docs) are expected to participate. If you cannot attend, please log your apologies and weekly updates in the shared notes beforehand.

---

## Meeting Structure

Our meetings are 60 minutes long and follow a two-part agenda:

### 1. Celebrations & Questions (15 minutes)
We go around the group using a shared agenda document. Each member provides:
* **Something to Share/Celebrate:** A successful simulation, a clean plot, a new package discovered, or a paper accepted.
* **A Technical Question/roadblock:** Seeking input on a bug, a dataset, or a modeling choice.

### 2. Main Session (45 minutes)
This slot rotates between different formats depending on the week:
* **Project Presentation:** A member shares their current research in detail (slides or a Jupyter notebook walk-through) to get group feedback.
* **Journal Club:** Discussing a recent high-impact paper in cheminformatics, structural bioinformatics, or molecular modeling.
* **Open Discussion/Hack Session:** Collaborating on a shared lab tool (e.g., MolMeDB features, ChannelsDB, workflows) or debugging a complex issue together.

---

## Roles: The Chair

To distribute leadership skills and ensure diverse perspectives, members rotate the role of **Meeting Chair**.

As the Chair for the week, your responsibilities are:
1. **Circulate the Agenda:** Send the link to the shared meeting note on MS Teams by Wednesday afternoon.
2. **Facilitate the Meeting:** Start on time, keep updates concise during the first 15 minutes, and moderate the main session.
3. **Log Actions:** Ensure any key decisions or follow-up actions (e.g., "create new GitHub issue for MolMeDB API fix") are recorded in the notes.

> [!NOTE]
> If you are a Bachelor's or Master's student and chairing a meeting feels intimidating, don't worry! More senior members and Karel will happily co-chair or mentor you through the process.
