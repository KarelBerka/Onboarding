# Blogging Exercise 3: Open Science & Code Contribution

* **Goal:** Demystify the process of contributing to open source software, databases, or public workflows.
* **Timeline:** Within your first six weeks of joining the lab.
* **Format:** A short Markdown write-up (approx. 500 words) describing your contribution, published in your progress log.

---

## The Brief
In the Chemical Data Lab, we are committed to **Open Science** and **FAIR Data**. Much of our work relies on open source tools (like GROMACS, PyMOL, RDKit) and databases (like MolMeDB, ChannelsDB). A key value of our group is giving back to the community by sharing code, improving documentation, and building open workflows.

The brief for this exercise is to **make a contribution to a public science project or repository and write about what you did, how you did it, and how it felt.**

> [!TIP]
> A "contribution" does not have to be a major feature! Valuable contributions include:
> * Fixing a typo or clarifying an API endpoint in the MolMeDB documentation.
> * Writing a unit test for a lab script.
> * Formatting a Jupyter notebook tutorial and sharing it publicly.
> * Publishing a computational workflow on **WorkflowHub**.
> * Submitting a bug report or feature request with a reproducible example on GitHub.

---

## Key Points to Cover in Your Summary
* **The Project:** Which repository or project did you choose, and why is it useful to computational chemists?
* **The Contribution:** What did you change or add (e.g., code, documentation, bug report)? Provide a link to your pull request or commit.
* **The Process:** How did you identify the issue? Did you discuss it with Karel or other lab members first?
* **The Reflection:** How did it feel to share your work publicly? What did you find challenging, and what advice would you give to others making their first contribution?
